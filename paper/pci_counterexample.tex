\title{PCI rings and V-domains are not left-right symmetric}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We construct a countable domain $R$ which is a left PCI domain---every proper cyclic left $R$-module is injective---but which is not right Ore. Consequently $R$ is neither a right V-ring nor a right PCI ring, while its opposite ring $R^{\op}$ is a right PCI domain which is not left PCI, not a left V-ring, and not left Ore. This answers, in the negative, questions of Cozzens and Faith, of Damiano, and of Jain, Lam and Leroy. The ring is an Ore extension $K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ in which $K$ is a free field in the sense of Cohn and $\sigma$ is a non-surjective endomorphism. The endomorphism and the derivation are prescribed on the free generators of $K$ in such a way that every linear equation governing the divisibility of a cyclic module $R/Rf$ is solved by a vector of free generators.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A ring $R$ is a \emph{right PCI ring} if every proper cyclic right $R$-module---that is, every cyclic right module not isomorphic to $R_R$---is injective, and a \emph{right V-ring} if every simple right $R$-module is injective; left PCI rings and left V-rings are defined symmetrically. Faith \cite{Fai73} showed that a right PCI ring is either semisimple Artinian or a simple right semihereditary right Ore domain, and Damiano \cite[Theorem~1]{Dam79} showed that in the second case it is right Noetherian. Examples of PCI domains (on both sides) were given by Cozzens \cite{Coz70}. Whether every right PCI ring is left PCI has been open since \cite{Fai73}; see \cite[\S2]{BG75}, \cite[\S7]{CF75} and \cite[\S2]{JS09}. Cozzens and Faith \cite[6.25, 6.26]{CF75}, using a theorem of Boyle \cite{Boy74}, showed that a right Noetherian right PCI domain which is left Ore is left PCI; since right Noetherianity is automatic by Damiano's theorem, the question is whether a right PCI domain must be left Ore. The companion question, whether a left V-domain must be a right V-domain, goes back to Cozzens and Faith \cite[\S7, (10)]{CF75} and is restated as open in \cite[\S1]{JLL09} and \cite[Remark~2.8]{BDV18}. Cozzens and Faith \cite[\S7, (7), (10)]{CF75} proposed to look for counterexamples among Ore extensions $K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ over a division ring $K$ with a non-surjective endomorphism $\sigma$. Jain, Lam and Leroy \cite[\S6]{JLL09} took this up: they showed that a left V-domain of this form is right PCI if and only if $\sigma$ is onto \cite[Theorem~6.2]{JLL09}, and remarked that they knew of no left V-domain $K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ with $\sigma$ not onto \cite[Remark~6.3]{JLL09}.

We produce such an example.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let $k$ be a countable commutative field. There are a countable division ring $K\supseteq k$, a non-surjective endomorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ and a $\sigma$-derivation $\delta$ of $K$ such that the Ore extension $R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is a simple principal left ideal domain and a left PCI domain; in particular $R$ is a left V-domain.
\item $R$ is not right Ore, not a right PCI ring, and not a right V-ring.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently $R^{\op}$ is a simple right Noetherian right hereditary right PCI domain which is not left Ore, not a left V-ring, and not left PCI.
\end{theorem}

The proof has two independent halves.

The first half (Section~\ref{sec:ore}) is elementary ring theory over an arbitrary division ring $K$ with an endomorphism $\sigma$ and a $\sigma$-derivation $\delta$. If $f\in R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ is monic of degree $n$, the cyclic module $R/Rf$ is the row space $K^n$ with $t$ acting by a pseudo-linear map $T_f$, and $R/Rf$ is injective as soon as the operators $g(T_f)$ on $K^n$, for non-zero $g\in R$, are all surjective; this is Baer's criterion, and it gives a criterion for $R$ to be left PCI (Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion}). If $\sigma$ is not surjective then $R$ is not right Ore (Proposition~\ref{prop:notore}), and a cardinality argument shows that a countable such $R$ has no non-zero countable injective right module, so is neither a right V-ring nor a right PCI ring (Lemma~\ref{lem:countable}). The Main Theorem is thereby reduced to Theorem~\ref{thm:target}: there is a countable $(K,\sigma,\delta)$ with $\sigma$ not surjective over which all the operators $g(T_f)$ are surjective.

The second half (Sections~\ref{sec:freefield} and~\ref{sec:construction}) constructs $(K,\sigma,\delta)$. We take $K$ to be Cohn's free field on a countable set $Y$ of generators. The only input from Cohn's theory is that the free algebra $\free Y$ has a universal field of fractions; from this we derive, following Cohn, that an endomorphism and a twisted derivation of $K$ may be prescribed arbitrarily on $Y$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:extend}), and that the endomorphism is not surjective if its restriction to $Y$ is not (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonsurj}). The construction itself rests on a single observation. If $x\in K^n$ is a vector whose coordinates are fixed by $\sigma$, then $T_f(x)=xC_f+\delta(x)$, where $C_f$ is the companion matrix of $f$; so to prescribe $\delta$ on the coordinates of $x$ is the same as to prescribe $T_f(x)$. For each equation $g(T_f)(v)=e$, with $f$ and $g$ monic of degrees $n$ and $m$, we therefore reserve $nm$ generators fixed by $\sigma$, arrange them into vectors $x_0,\dots,x_{m-1}\in K^n$, and decree that $T_f(x_j)=x_{j+1}$ for $j<m-1$ and $T_f(x_{m-1})=e-\sum_{j<m}g_jx_j$; then $g(T_f)(x_0)=e$. These prescriptions involve only the coefficients of $f$, $g$ and $e$, and not $\sigma$ or $\delta$ themselves, so all of them can be imposed at once. No tower of fields and no compatibility conditions are needed.

\subsection*{Conventions}
Rings have an identity, and homomorphisms preserve it; $k$ is a commutative field. For a division ring $K$, an \emph{endomorphism} $\sigma$ of $K$ is a ring endomorphism (automatically injective), and a \emph{$\sigma$-derivation} of $K$ is an additive map $\delta\colon K\to K$ with
\[
\delta(ab)=\sigma(a)\delta(b)+\delta(a)b\qquad(a,b\in K).
\]
A \emph{division subring} of a division ring is a subring closed under inverses of non-zero elements. For a map $\alpha$ defined on $K$ and a row vector or matrix $v$ over $K$, we write $\alpha(v)$ for the entrywise image. Compositions of maps are written by juxtaposition: $\phi\psi=\phi\circ\psi$.

\section{Ore extensions over division rings}\label{sec:ore}

Throughout this section $K$ is a division ring with an endomorphism $\sigma$ and a $\sigma$-derivation $\delta$. The \emph{Ore extension} $R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ is the ring which is a free left $K$-module with basis $1,t,t^2,\dots$ and whose multiplication is determined by
\[
ta=\sigma(a)t+\delta(a)\qquad(a\in K).
\]
Its elements are written $f=\sum_if_it^i$ with the coefficients on the left, and $\deg f$ is the usual degree. Since $\sigma$ is injective, $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$; so $R$ is a domain, and its units are the non-zero elements of $K$. There is a left division algorithm: for $f\ne0$, every $r\in R$ can be written uniquely as $r=qf+b$ with $\deg b<\deg f$ (compare \cite[Theorem~1.3.2]{CohFIR}, where the coefficients are written on the right). Hence every left ideal of $R$ is of the form $Rf$, and $R$ is a \emph{principal left ideal domain}; in particular $R$ is left Noetherian and left hereditary. Since $R$ is a domain, $Rf\cong R$ as left $R$-modules whenever $f\ne0$.

\subsection{The modules $R/Rf$}
Let $f=t^n+f_{n-1}t^{n-1}+\dots+f_0\in R$ be monic of degree $n\ge1$. By the division algorithm, the classes of $1,t,\dots,t^{n-1}$ form a basis of $R/Rf$ as a left $K$-vector space, and we identify $R/Rf$ with the space $K^n$ of row vectors via
\[
\sum_{i<n}b_it^i+Rf\ \longmapsto\ (b_0,\dots,b_{n-1}).
\]
Let $C_f\in\M_n(K)$ be the companion matrix of $f$, whose rows are $e_2,\dots,e_n,(-f_0,\dots,-f_{n-1})$, where $e_1,\dots,e_n$ are the standard basis vectors. Define
\[
T_f\colon K^n\to K^n,\qquad T_f(v)=\sigma(v)C_f+\delta(v).
\]
The map $T_f$ is additive and \emph{pseudo-linear}: $T_f(av)=\sigma(a)T_f(v)+\delta(a)v$ for $a\in K$. For $g=\sum_{i=0}^mg_it^i\in R$ we put
\[
g(T_f)(v)=\sum_{i=0}^mg_i\,T_f^{\,i}(v)\qquad(v\in K^n),
\]
where $T_f^{\,i}$ is the $i$-fold iterate of $T_f$ and each $g_i$ acts by left scalar multiplication. The same definitions make sense over any division ring $K'\supseteq K$ to which $\sigma$ and $\delta$ have been extended, and then $T_f$ on $K'^n$ restricts to $T_f$ on $K^n$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:action}
Under the identification $R/Rf=K^n$, left multiplication by $g\in R$ on $R/Rf$ is the map $g(T_f)$. In particular, for $b=\sum_{i<n}b_it^i$ and $c=\sum_{i<n}c_it^i$,
\[
gb\equiv c\pmod{Rf}\iff g(T_f)(b_0,\dots,b_{n-1})=(c_0,\dots,c_{n-1}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Left multiplication by $a\in K$ is scalar multiplication by $a$. Left multiplication by $t$ sends $\sum_{i<n}b_it^i$ to $\sum_{i<n}\sigma(b_i)t^{i+1}+\sum_{i<n}\delta(b_i)t^i$, and $t^n\equiv-\sum_{j<n}f_jt^j\pmod{Rf}$; so its coefficient vector is $\sigma(b)C_f+\delta(b)=T_f(b)$, where $b=(b_0,\dots,b_{n-1})$. Iterating, $t^i$ acts by $T_f^{\,i}$, and summing, $g$ acts by $g(T_f)$.
\end{proof}

This is the identification of $R/Rf$ with the pseudo-linear space $(K^n,T_f)$ used in \cite[\S4]{JLL09}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:criterion}
Suppose that for all monic $f,g\in R$ of degree $\ge1$ the map $g(T_f)\colon K^n\to K^n$, where $n=\deg f$, is surjective. Then $R$ is a simple ring, a left PCI domain and a left V-domain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$R$ is left PCI.} A cyclic left $R$-module is $R/Rf$ for some $f\in R$. If $f=0$ this is $R$ itself. If $f\ne0$ it is finite-dimensional over $K$, whereas $R$ is not, so $R/Rf$ is a proper cyclic module, and we must show that it is injective. Since $Rf=Ra^{-1}f$ for $a\in K^\times$, we may take $f$ monic; and since $R/Rf=0$ if $\deg f=0$, we may take $\deg f=n\ge1$. Put $M=R/Rf$. By Baer's criterion, $M$ is injective if and only if every homomorphism from a left ideal of $R$ to $M$ extends to $R$. The left ideals are the $Rg$, and for $g\ne0$ the module $Rg$ is free on $g$, so a homomorphism $Rg\to M$ is the same as an element $m\in M$ (the image of $g$), and it extends to $R$ if and only if $m=gm'$ for some $m'\in M$. Thus $M$ is injective if and only if $gM=M$ for every $g\ne0$. Write $g=ag_1$ with $a\in K^\times$ and $g_1$ monic; then $gM=g_1M$. If $\deg g_1=0$ then $g_1=1$; if $\deg g_1\ge1$ then $g_1M=M$ says, by Lemma~\ref{lem:action}, that $g_1(T_f)$ is surjective on $K^n$, which is the hypothesis.

\emph{$R$ is simple.} Let $I\ne0$ be a two-sided ideal and $0\ne f\in I$. Multiplying by the inverse of the leading coefficient we may take $f$ monic. If $\deg f=0$ then $f=1\in I$. Otherwise $f(R/Rf)=R/Rf$ by what has just been proved, so $fb\equiv1\pmod{Rf}$ for some $b\in R$, and $1\in fR+Rf\subseteq I$.

\emph{$R$ is left V.} A simple left $R$-module is $R/L$ for a maximal left ideal $L$. The zero ideal is not maximal, since $Rt$ is a non-zero proper left ideal; so $L\ne0$, and $R/L$ is a proper cyclic module, hence injective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The right side}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:notore}
If $c\in K\setminus\sigma(K)$, then $tR\cap ctR=0$. In particular, if $\sigma$ is not surjective then $R$ is not right Ore.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $tr=ctr'$ with $r,r'\ne0$. Comparing degrees, $\deg r=\deg r'=p$, say, with leading coefficients $r_p$ and $r'_p$. The leading coefficient of $tr$ is $\sigma(r_p)$ and that of $ctr'$ is $c\sigma(r'_p)$, so $c=\sigma(r_p)\sigma(r'_p)^{-1}=\sigma(r_pr_p'^{-1})\in\sigma(K)$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The same computation appears in the proof of \cite[Proposition~6.1]{JLL09}, where it is used to show that $R_R$ is not uniform. A right PCI domain is right Ore \cite[6.17]{CF75}, so Proposition~\ref{prop:notore} already shows that $R$ is not right PCI when $\sigma$ is not surjective. For the failure of the right V-property we use a cardinality argument in the spirit of Lawrence \cite[Theorem~4]{Law77}; it also gives the failure of the right PCI-property without appeal to \cite{CF75}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:countable}
Let $R$ be a countable domain containing non-zero elements $a,b$ with $aR\cap bR=0$. Then no non-zero countable right $R$-module is injective. Consequently $R$ is neither a right V-ring nor a right PCI ring.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The right ideals $b^iaR$ ($i\in\N$) are independent. Indeed, if $\sum_{i=0}^Nb^iar_i=0$, then
\[
ar_0=-b\sum_{i=1}^Nb^{i-1}ar_i\in aR\cap bR=0,
\]
so $r_0=0$ because $R$ is a domain; cancelling $b$ on the left and repeating gives $r_i=0$ for all $i$. Each $b^iaR$ is isomorphic to $R_R$, so $I=\bigoplus_{i\in\N}b^iaR$ is a free right ideal of countably infinite rank. If $E$ is an injective right $R$-module, the restriction map $\Hom_R(R,E)\to\Hom_R(I,E)$ is surjective; as $\Hom_R(R,E)\cong E$ and $\Hom_R(I,E)\cong E^{\N}$, this gives $|E^{\N}|\le|E|$, which is impossible if $E$ is non-zero and countable, since then $|E^{\N}|=2^{\aleph_0}$.

A simple right $R$-module $R/M$ is countable, hence not injective; so $R$ is not a right V-ring. Moreover $R/M$ is a proper cyclic module: $R_R$ is not simple, since $aR$ is a non-zero right ideal and $aR\ne R$ (otherwise $b\in aR\cap bR$), so $R/M\not\cong R_R$. Hence $R$ is not a right PCI ring either.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reduction}
The Main Theorem is now reduced to the following statement, which is proved in Section~\ref{sec:construction}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:target}
Let $k$ be a countable commutative field. There are a countable division ring $K\supseteq k$, a non-surjective endomorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ and a $\sigma$-derivation $\delta$ of $K$ such that, in $R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$, for all monic $f,g\in R$ of degree $\ge1$ the map $g(T_f)\colon K^n\to K^n$, $n=\deg f$, is surjective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the Main Theorem from Theorem~\ref{thm:target}]
Let $K$, $\sigma$ and $\delta$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:target}, and let $R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$. Then $R$ is a countable principal left ideal domain, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion} it is simple, left PCI and left V. Choose $c\in K\setminus\sigma(K)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:notore} we have $tR\cap ctR=0$, so $R$ is not right Ore; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:countable}, applied with $a=t$ and $b=ct$, it is neither a right V-ring nor a right PCI ring. The statements about $R^{\op}$ are the mirror images, using that a principal left ideal domain is left Noetherian and left hereditary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:answers}
Let $R$ be the ring of the Main Theorem.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A right PCI ring need not be left PCI \textup{(}\cite{Fai73}, \cite[\S7, (10)]{CF75}, \cite{Dam79}\textup{)}: $R^{\op}$.
\item A right PCI domain, and a right V-domain, need not be left Ore \textup{(}\cite[\S7, (7)]{CF75}\textup{)}: $R^{\op}$.
\item A left V-domain need not be a right V-domain \textup{(}\cite[\S7, (10)]{CF75}, \cite[\S1]{JLL09}, \cite[Remark~2.8]{BDV18}\textup{)}: $R$.
\item There is a left V-domain of the form $K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ with $\sigma$ not onto \textup{(}\cite[Remark~6.3]{JLL09}\textup{)}: $R$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\section{Free fields}\label{sec:freefield}

We recall the little that we need from Cohn's theory of universal fields of fractions \cite[Chapter~4 and \S5.4]{CohSF}, \cite[Chapter~7]{CohFIR}. A square matrix $A$ of size $p$ over a ring $S$ is \emph{full} if it cannot be written as $A=PQ$ with $P$ of size $p\times r$, $Q$ of size $r\times p$ and $r<p$. Over a domain, a $1\times1$ matrix $(a)$ is full if and only if $a\ne0$. A ring homomorphism $\phi\colon S\to B$ is \emph{fully inverting} if for every full matrix $A$ over $S$ the matrix $\phi(A)$ is invertible over $B$.

Fix a set $Y$ and let $S=\free Y$ be the free associative $k$-algebra on $Y$. We use one theorem of Cohn: the free algebra $S$ is a fir \cite[Corollary~2.5.2]{CohFIR}, and the universal localization of a fir (or semifir) at the set of all its full matrices is a division ring \cite[Theorem~4.5.8, Corollary~4.5.9]{CohSF}, \cite[Theorem~7.5.13]{CohFIR}. Together with the defining property of a universal localization \cite[\S4.1]{CohSF}, this says the following.

\begin{theorem}[Cohn]\label{thm:cohn}
There are a division ring $\ff Y$ and a fully inverting homomorphism $\lambda\colon S\to\ff Y$ such that every fully inverting homomorphism $\phi\colon S\to B$ into an arbitrary ring $B$ factors uniquely as $\phi=\phi'\lambda$ with $\phi'\colon\ff Y\to B$ a ring homomorphism.
\end{theorem}

The division ring $\ff Y$ is the \emph{universal field of fractions} of $S$; we call it the \emph{free field} on $Y$ over $k$. The map $\lambda$ is injective, since $(a)$ is full for $a\ne0$, and we regard $S$ as a subring of $\ff Y$, writing $y$ for $\lambda(y)$ when no confusion can arise. The universal property has the following consequence, which does most of the work in this section.

\begin{lemma}[rigidity]\label{lem:rigid}
Let $\phi,\psi\colon\ff Y\to B$ be ring homomorphisms into a ring $B$. If $\phi\lambda=\psi\lambda$, then $\phi=\psi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a full matrix $A$ over $S$, the matrix $\lambda(A)$ is invertible over $\ff Y$, so $\phi\lambda(A)$ is invertible over $B$. Thus $\phi\lambda$ is fully inverting, and $\phi$ and $\psi$ are both factorizations of it through $\lambda$; they coincide by the uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:cohn}.
\end{proof}

For instance, $k$ is central in $\ff Y$: for $c\in k^\times$, conjugation by $c$ is an automorphism of $\ff Y$ which agrees with the identity on $S$, hence is the identity. So $\ff Y$ is a $k$-algebra, and every $k$-algebra homomorphism out of $S$ is determined by its values on $Y$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:generated}
$\ff Y$ is generated by $Y$ as a division ring. In particular, if $k$ and $Y$ are countable, so is $\ff Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $D\subseteq\ff Y$ be the division subring generated by $S$. A square matrix over $D$ which is invertible over $\ff Y$ is invertible over $D$: its rows are left linearly independent over $\ff Y$, hence over $D$, and a square matrix over a division ring with linearly independent rows is invertible. Hence the corestriction $S\to D$ of $\lambda$ is fully inverting, and it factors as $\psi\lambda$ with $\psi\colon\ff Y\to D$. The composite of $\psi$ with the inclusion $D\subseteq\ff Y$ agrees with the identity on $S$, so it is the identity by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, and $D=\ff Y$. If $k$ and $Y$ are countable then so is $S$, and the division subring generated by a countable set is countable.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:generated} is used only to control the cardinality of $\ff Y$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:honest}
Let $\phi\colon S\to S$ be a ring endomorphism with a left inverse, that is, a ring endomorphism $\rho$ of $S$ with $\rho\phi=\id_S$. Then $\phi$ maps full matrices to full matrices, and $\lambda\phi\colon S\to\ff Y$ is fully inverting.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $A$ is full of size $p$ and $\phi(A)=PQ$ with $P$ of size $p\times r$, $Q$ of size $r\times p$ and $r<p$, then $A=\rho\phi(A)=\rho(P)\rho(Q)$, contradicting the fullness of $A$. So $\phi(A)$ is full, and $\lambda\phi(A)$ is invertible over $\ff Y$. (This is \cite[Proposition~4.5.1]{CohSF}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[endomorphisms and derivations prescribed on generators]\label{prop:extend}
Let $s\colon Y\to Y$ be an injective map and $d\colon Y\to\ff Y$ an arbitrary map. There are an endomorphism $\sigma$ of $\ff Y$ and a $\sigma$-derivation $\delta$ of $\ff Y$ with
\[
\sigma(y)=s(y),\qquad\delta(y)=d(y)\qquad(y\in Y).
\]
Moreover, if $\hat s$ denotes the $k$-algebra endomorphism of $S$ induced by $s$, then $\sigma\lambda=\lambda\hat s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $U=\ff Y$.

\emph{Step 1: $\lambda\hat s$ is fully inverting.} Since $s$ is injective, there is a $k$-algebra endomorphism $\rho$ of $S$ with $\rho(s(y))=y$ for $y\in Y$ and $\rho(y')=0$ for $y'\in Y\setminus s(Y)$. Then $\rho\hat s$ is the identity on $Y$, hence on $S$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:honest} applies.

\emph{Step 2: a homomorphism into triangular matrices.} Let $\UT(U)$ be the ring of upper triangular $2\times2$ matrices over $U$; it is a $k$-algebra since $k$ is central in $U$, and the two diagonal entries $\pi_1,\pi_2\colon\UT(U)\to U$ are ring homomorphisms. Let $\theta\colon S\to\UT(U)$ be the $k$-algebra homomorphism with
\[
\theta(y)=\begin{pmatrix}s(y)&d(y)\\0&y\end{pmatrix}\qquad(y\in Y).
\]
Then $\pi_1\theta=\lambda\hat s$ and $\pi_2\theta=\lambda$, since both sides are $k$-algebra homomorphisms $S\to U$ that agree on $Y$.

\emph{Step 3: $\theta$ is fully inverting.} Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a full $p\times p$ matrix over $S$. The matrix $\theta(A)\in\M_p(\UT(U))$ has entries $\theta(a_{ij})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\hat s(a_{ij})&q_{ij}\\0&a_{ij}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for certain $q_{ij}\in U$. Regard it as a $2p\times2p$ matrix over $U$ whose rows and columns are indexed by pairs $(i,\epsilon)$ with $1\le i\le p$ and $\epsilon\in\{1,2\}$, and reorder the rows and columns so that those with $\epsilon=1$ come first. This reordering is an isomorphism of $\M_p(\M_2(U))$ onto $\M_{2p}(U)$ which maps $\M_p(\UT(U))$ onto the ring of block upper triangular matrices with $p\times p$ blocks, and it sends $\theta(A)$ to
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\hat s(A)&Q\\0&\lambda(A)\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q=(q_{ij}).
\]
The diagonal blocks are invertible over $U$: $\lambda(A)$ because $\lambda$ is fully inverting, and $\lambda\hat s(A)$ by Step~1. Hence this block matrix is invertible, with inverse
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\hat s(A)^{-1}&-\lambda\hat s(A)^{-1}Q\lambda(A)^{-1}\\0&\lambda(A)^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is again block upper triangular. So $\theta(A)$ is invertible in $\M_p(\UT(U))$, and $\theta$ is fully inverting. By Theorem~\ref{thm:cohn}, $\theta=\Theta\lambda$ for a ring homomorphism $\Theta\colon U\to\UT(U)$.

\emph{Step 4: reading off $\sigma$ and $\delta$.} The homomorphism $\pi_2\Theta\colon U\to U$ satisfies $\pi_2\Theta\lambda=\pi_2\theta=\lambda$, so $\pi_2\Theta=\id_U$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}. We may therefore write
\[
\Theta(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma(u)&\delta(u)\\0&u\end{pmatrix}\qquad(u\in U)
\]
for maps $\sigma,\delta\colon U\to U$. That $\Theta$ is additive says that $\sigma$ and $\delta$ are additive; that $\Theta(1)$ is the identity matrix says that $\sigma(1)=1$ and $\delta(1)=0$; and comparing the entries of $\Theta(uv)=\Theta(u)\Theta(v)$ gives
\[
\sigma(uv)=\sigma(u)\sigma(v),\qquad\delta(uv)=\sigma(u)\delta(v)+\delta(u)v .
\]
So $\sigma$ is an endomorphism of $U$ and $\delta$ is a $\sigma$-derivation (this is the standard description of twisted derivations by triangular matrices, \cite[\S2.1]{CohSF}). Finally, $\Theta\lambda=\theta$ gives $\sigma(y)=s(y)$ and $\delta(y)=d(y)$ for $y\in Y$, and $\sigma\lambda=\pi_1\Theta\lambda=\pi_1\theta=\lambda\hat s$.
\end{proof}

The argument is that of \cite[Theorem~7.5.17]{CohFIR}, which extends ordinary derivations to universal fields of fractions, with a twist by $\sigma$ inserted.

\begin{lemma}[non-surjectivity]\label{lem:nonsurj}
In the situation of Proposition~\ref{prop:extend}, let $y_0\in Y\setminus s(Y)$. Then $y_0\notin\sigma(\ff Y)$. In particular $\sigma$ is not surjective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $U=\ff Y$. Let $\hat\tau$ be the $k$-algebra automorphism of $S$ with $\hat\tau(y_0)=y_0+1$ and $\hat\tau(y)=y$ for $y\ne y_0$. Its inverse is a left inverse, so $\lambda\hat\tau$ is fully inverting by Lemma~\ref{lem:honest}, and it factors as $\tau\lambda$ for an endomorphism $\tau$ of $U$. Since $y_0\notin s(Y)$, the endomorphism $\hat\tau$ fixes every element of $s(Y)$, so $\hat\tau\hat s=\hat s$. Hence
\[
\tau\sigma\lambda=\tau\lambda\hat s=\lambda\hat\tau\hat s=\lambda\hat s=\sigma\lambda,
\]
and $\tau\sigma=\sigma$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}. Thus $\tau$ fixes $\sigma(U)$ pointwise, whereas $\tau(y_0)=y_0+1\ne y_0$. So $y_0\notin\sigma(U)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Cohn uses precisely this set-up in \cite[\S5]{Coh77} and \cite[\S5.9]{CohSF}: a free algebra on generators indexed by $\Lambda\times\N^2$, an endomorphism shifting one index (which has a left inverse and therefore preserves full matrices) and a derivation shifting the other, both extended to the free field. In \cite{Coh77} the non-surjectivity of the extended endomorphism is stated to be clear; Lemma~\ref{lem:nonsurj} supplies the argument.
\end{remark}

\section{The construction}\label{sec:construction}

\begin{construction}\label{constr}
Let $k$ be a countable commutative field. Let $Z$ be a countably infinite set, let
\[
Y=\{y_i:i\in\N\}\sqcup Z,
\]
and let $K=\ff Y$ be the free field on $Y$ over $k$; it is countable by Lemma~\ref{lem:generated}. Let $s\colon Y\to Y$ be the map with $s(y_i)=y_{i+1}$ and $s(z)=z$ for $z\in Z$. It is injective, and $y_0\notin s(Y)$.

A \emph{task} is a triple $\tau=(f,g,e)$ with
\[
f=(f_0,\dots,f_{n-1})\in K^n,\qquad g=(g_0,\dots,g_{m-1})\in K^m,\qquad e\in K^n,
\]
where $n,m\ge1$. We think of $f$ and $g$ as the coefficient lists of the monic polynomials $t^n+\sum_{i<n}f_it^i$ and $t^m+\sum_{j<m}g_jt^j$, and of $e$ as a right-hand side; but note that a task is defined in terms of $K$ alone, before $\sigma$ and $\delta$ have been chosen. Likewise the companion matrix $C_f\in\M_n(K)$ depends only on the list $f$. The set $\T$ of all tasks is countable, since $K$ is.

Because $\T$ is countable and $Z$ is infinite, we may choose, for each task $\tau=(f,g,e)$ with $f\in K^n$ and $g\in K^m$, a subset
\[
X_\tau=\{x^\tau_{l,j}:1\le l\le n,\ 0\le j\le m-1\}\subseteq Z
\]
of $nm$ elements, in such a way that the sets $X_\tau$ ($\tau\in\T$) are pairwise disjoint. For each $\tau$ and $0\le j\le m-1$ let $x^\tau_j=(x^\tau_{1,j},\dots,x^\tau_{n,j})\in K^n$ be the vector of generators in the $j$th column of $X_\tau$.

Define $d\colon Y\to K$ as follows. On $\{y_i\}$ and on $Z\setminus\bigcup_\tau X_\tau$ let $d=0$. On $X_\tau$, for $\tau=(f,g,e)$, writing $x_j=x^\tau_j$ and $C=C_f$, let $d$ be given in vector form by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d}
d(x_j)=x_{j+1}-x_jC\quad(0\le j\le m-2),\qquad d(x_{m-1})=e-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_jx_j-x_{m-1}C ;
\end{equation}
that is, $d(x^\tau_{l,j})$ is the $l$th coordinate of the right-hand side. These are elements of $K$, and since the sets $X_\tau$ are disjoint, $d$ is well defined.

Finally, let $(\sigma,\delta)$ be an endomorphism and a $\sigma$-derivation of $K$ with $\sigma(y)=s(y)$ and $\delta(y)=d(y)$ for $y\in Y$, as provided by Proposition~\ref{prop:extend}, and let $R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]$.
\end{construction}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:target}]
We show that $K$, $\sigma$, $\delta$ of Construction~\ref{constr} have the required properties. $K$ is countable, and $\sigma$ is not surjective by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonsurj}, applied to $y_0\notin s(Y)$.

Now let $f,g\in R$ be monic of degrees $n,m\ge1$ and let $e\in K^n$; we must find $v\in K^n$ with $g(T_f)(v)=e$. Let $\tau=(f,g,e)\in\T$ be the task formed by the coefficient lists of $f$ and $g$ and by $e$, and write $x_j=x^\tau_j$ and $C=C_f$. The coordinates of each $x_j$ lie in $Z$, so they are fixed by $\sigma$, and $\delta$ acts on them by $d$. Hence
\[
T_f(x_j)=\sigma(x_j)C+\delta(x_j)=x_jC+d(x_j)\qquad(0\le j\le m-1),
\]
and by \eqref{eq:d},
\[
T_f(x_j)=x_{j+1}\quad(0\le j\le m-2),\qquad T_f(x_{m-1})=e-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_jx_j .
\]
By induction, $T_f^{\,j}(x_0)=x_j$ for $0\le j\le m-1$, and then $T_f^{\,m}(x_0)=T_f(x_{m-1})=e-\sum_{j<m}g_jx_j$. Since $g$ is monic of degree $m$,
\[
g(T_f)(x_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j\,T_f^{\,j}(x_0)+T_f^{\,m}(x_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_jx_j+\Bigl(e-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_jx_j\Bigr)=e .
\]
So $v=x_0$ solves the equation.
\end{proof}

This completes the proof of the Main Theorem.

\section{Remarks}\label{sec:remarks}

\begin{remark}[on the shape of the construction]
Two features of the construction are forced.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The derivation cannot be omitted or made inner. If $\delta=0$, then $tR\subseteq Rt$ and $Rt$ is a proper two-sided ideal of $K[t;\sigma]$, so the ring is not simple. If $\delta$ is inner, $\delta(a)=ca-\sigma(a)c$ for some $c\in K$, then the substitution $t'=t-c$ gives $K[t;\sigma,\delta]=K[t';\sigma]$. In particular $K$ cannot be commutative: if $K$ is commutative and $\sigma\ne\id$, every $\sigma$-derivation is inner, because for $b$ with $\sigma(b)\ne b$ the identity $\delta(ab)=\delta(ba)$ yields $\delta(a)=c(a-\sigma(a))$ with $c=\delta(b)(b-\sigma(b))^{-1}$ \cite[\S2]{Coh77}.
\item The solutions of the equations $g(T_f)(v)=e$ have to be adjoined as free generators. What makes the construction work is that on the free field the values of $\sigma$ and $\delta$ on the generators are subject to no constraints whatsoever (Proposition~\ref{prop:extend}); an Ore extension or a crossed product of $K$ would impose commutation relations between the new elements and $K$ which $\delta$ would have to respect.
\end{enumerate}
By contrast, the choice to fix the new generators under $\sigma$, and to prescribe $T_f$ rather than $\delta$ on them, is a matter of convenience, but a decisive one. If one prescribes $\delta(x_j)=x_{j+1}$ directly, one finds $T_f^{\,i}(x_0)=\sum_{j\le i}x_jN_{i,j}$ for matrices $N_{i,j}$ built from $\sigma$ and $\delta$ applied to the coefficients of $f$, so the required value of $\delta$ on the last generator depends on $\sigma$ and $\delta$, and one is led to build $K$ as the union of a tower of free fields, each solving the equations of the previous one, with the attendant compatibility checks. Prescribing $T_f(x_j)$ makes the required values of $d$ depend only on the coefficients of $f$, $g$ and $e$, so that a single free field suffices.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[uncountable $k$]
For an arbitrary commutative field $k$, take $|Z|=\max(|k|,\aleph_0)$ in Construction~\ref{constr}. Then $|K|=|Z|$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:generated}, so $|\T|=|Z|$ and the sets $X_\tau$ can still be chosen, and the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:target} goes through unchanged. One obtains a left PCI domain $K[t;\sigma,\delta]$ which is not right Ore, hence not right PCI by \cite[6.17]{CF75}. Only the failure of the right V-property was derived from countability.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[on the literature used]
Apart from Baer's criterion and the elementary theory of Ore extensions, the proof uses from the literature only Theorem~\ref{thm:cohn}: the free algebra has a universal field of fractions with the universal property of a localization. Lemmas~\ref{lem:rigid}--\ref{lem:honest}, Proposition~\ref{prop:extend} and Lemma~\ref{lem:nonsurj} are derived from it. In particular, Cohn's theorem that honest homomorphisms between semifirs extend to their universal fields of fractions \cite[Theorem~4.5.10]{CohSF} is not needed: the extensions used here are obtained directly from the universal property, and the identifications between free fields on different sets of generators that a tower would require do not arise.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[further properties of $R$]
By \cite[Theorem~6.2]{JLL09}, the maximal right quotient ring of the left V-domain $R$ is directly infinite, and by \cite[Proposition~6.1]{JLL09} no cyclic right module $R/fR$ with $f\ne0$ is injective and every principal right ideal of $R$ is closed. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:countable} shows more than was used: every non-zero injective right $R$-module $E$ satisfies $|E|^{\aleph_0}\le|E|$, hence $|E|\ge2^{\aleph_0}$.
\end{remark}
